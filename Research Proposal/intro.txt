1.1	Background & Literature Survey

The poultry industry has been one of the leading contributors to the global livestock sector because it provides protein through both meat and eggs. The rapidly growing population, increasing consumer demand, and urbanization put pressure on poultry producers for productivity improvement with cost efficiency. Feed management has come out as an important determinant of biological and economic performances in poultry production. Since the cost of feed generally represents 60-70% of the total production expense in poultry farming, this makes it the largest operating expense; hence, any slight improvement in the efficiency of utilizing feed will create a large positive gain towards profitability as well as sustainability.

Standardized nutrient profiles such as crude protein, energy, and even essential amino acids are what most traditional feed formulation strategies rely on. Formulas are based more on general guidelines, for example, NRC standards rather than specific requirements of each flock. However, the growth of poultry is determined by several interacting factors among which include feeding behavior and metabolic efficiency as well as genetic variability. This is what happens when different flocks are fed with a unified feeding plan—there will be wastage on the part of some flocks and inadequate productivity from others in terms of poor weight gain and poor feed conversion ratio (FCR).
